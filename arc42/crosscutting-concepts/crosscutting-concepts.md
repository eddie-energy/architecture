The EDDIE System is designed to foster seamless interoperability in energy data exchanges by utilizing advanced models, standards, and data-sharing techniques. It integrates certain key concepts such as CIM (Common Information Model) and Dataspace principles, with a focus on processes and components specific to the energy sector.

## "Under-the-Hood" Technical Concepts  
- Common Information Model (CIM) Integration:  
  - The Interoperable Communication Component of the EDDIE Framework translates incoming data into CIM, ensuring semantic interoperability.  
  - The CIM-based Master Data Model enables consistent data processing and exchange.  
- **Dataspace**:  
  - General-purpose data spaces like Gaia-X and IDSA provide frameworks for cross-domain data sharing. They ensure interoperability, security, and sovereignty of data across various domains.  
  - A general-purpose data space is used as a foundation, but it is not fully implemented. Instead, the focus is on a domain-specific data space tailored for the energy sector. This approach includes domain-specific processes and components designed to handle energy data exchanges. While general-purpose data spaces are broad and flexible, they are not directly applicable to the specific needs of energy data. An equivalent ecosystem has been developed to meet the unique requirements of the energy sector.  
- **SAREF (Smart Appliances REFerence ontology)**:  
  - SAREF is a semantic data model developed by ETSI to standardize smart appliances within the IoT ecosystem, enabling interoperability across devices like household appliances, lighting systems, and HVAC systems.  
  - In the EDDIE system, SAREF can ensure interoperability and consistency of energy consumption data from smart appliances, enhancing the overall energy data management. The use of SAREF aligns with our approach to ensuring consistent data semantics across various devices.  
  - Alternatives to SAREF, such as **OneM2M** and **Brick Schema**, also provide standards for interoperability, but SAREF’s focus on smart appliances makes it particularly relevant to energy data exchanges in our context.  
- **SGAM (Smart Grid Architecture Model)**:  
  - SGAM is a reference architecture that organizes and describes the components of a smart grid system, covering views such as Business, Function, Information, Communication, Component, and Deployment.  
  - In the EDDIE system, SGAM can serve as a useful reference for organizing workflows, scenarios, and data models. The use of SGAM ensures that our approach to smart grid and energy data management remains consistent with global standards.  
  - Alternative models to SGAM, such as the **NIST Smart Grid Framework** and **IEC 61850**, offer similar ways to organize and describe smart grid components, providing additional perspectives on smart grid system development.
